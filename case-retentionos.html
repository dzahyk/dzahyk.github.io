WHERE IT STARTED

A retention spec that could not survive its own numbers.

RetentionOS started as my own product spec for fixed-broadband business customers. Before building it, I reviewed the spec and found 39 gaps — 11 of them critical — including no churn label, churn probability stored as an input, and an ROI formula that rewarded discounting everyone.

The rebuilt version answers three daily questions for a retention team: who is likely to leave in the next 60 days, why, and whether a discount or upgrade is worth its cost.

From the gap analysis

The mockup’s “net saved Rp840 million” for an account paying Rp45 million a month is impossible; the upper bound is about Rp396 million.

WHAT I CHANGED

Four fixes to my own spec.

Each one closes a gap from that review.

  1. 01

    Protected value, not full CLV

    An offer is worth the drop in churn probability times customer value, and the discount is paid by everyone who stays — so blind discounting loses money on paper.

  2. 02

    Evaluation in the open

    Holdout by account and time, grouped cross-validation, confidence intervals, a naive-rule baseline, and calibration are all shown, not a single accuracy number.

  3. 03

    Four-eyes approval

    Large or unrecommended offers wait for a retention manager. Requesters cannot approve their own offers, and a rejection needs a reason.

  4. 04

    Nothing goes to the customer

    The system only drafts messages. The original spec’s “apply offer & email” button had no control, so it was removed.

THE APP

Four views around one call queue.

A radar with a work queue ranked by revenue at risk, an account panel (summary, evidence, simulation, message), cohort survival and RFM migration, an accuracy page, and an approval log. The guide page opens on the product name set behind a cut-out miniature town; as you scroll the town rises over the name, and all motion switches off when the device asks for reduced motion.

Two people work from the queue:

UserMain question
Account managerWhich of my accounts should I call first, and what should I offer?
Retention managerIs this incentive worth its cost, and should I approve it?
TESTS & LIMITS

85 tests, and only 16 churn events to test against.

85 tests cover data and model, analytics and ROI, API contracts and security hardening, approval rules, and the factual accuracy of drafted messages — for example, that “no open tickets” is never written when ticket data is missing.

  • Not yet: an A/B test of real offer effects, or accuracy on real customer data.
  • Only 16 churn events in the test set, so confidence intervals are wide. There is no authentication: the role picker is a demo mode.
Accounts are generated with a fixed seed; no real customer or operator data is used.
