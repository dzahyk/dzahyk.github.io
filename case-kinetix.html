WHY IT EXISTS

Supplier risk shows up only when the warehouse runs low.

In many factories, supplier performance lives in a purchase-order spreadsheet while stock lives in another file. A supplier that is often late looks like a problem only once stock is already thin, and moving orders to another supplier is decided without numbers.

The goal: one PO file in, and within minutes the user knows which supplier to review, which material will run out, and what a partial switch would change.

From the product spec

Supplier performance and stock sit in different places, so risk becomes visible only after stock runs low.

HOW IT TOOK SHAPE

Question, sketch, then build.

Before any chart, I write down who will read it and what they have to decide. For Kinetix, that was a procurement analyst with one purchase-order file.

The question

Which supplier needs attention first, and why?

StepWhat happened
01 · QuestionOne reader, one file, one decision to support: which supplier to review first.
02 · SketchThree decisions on paper first. Clear: one score — delivery 40%, quality 35%, cost 25% — with every part on screen. Bold: a reorder line that moves with each supplier’s delays. Useful: a split test that only compares suppliers in the same category.
03 · BuildThe notes became Kinetix: a procurement workspace where every supplier score traces back to its purchase orders.

A working prototype on synthetic data; no claimed business results.

THE WORKSPACE

Six modules, one purchase-order file.

Overview, allocation simulator, PPIC inventory, anomaly log, supplier-letter copilot, and decision tools — plus a CSV import that accepts Indonesian and English column names.

TRADE-OFFS

Less clever, more checkable.

Four places where I picked numbers people can check over a smarter-looking model.

  1. 01

    A score you can take apart

    The composite score weights delivery 40%, quality 35%, and cost 25%, and every sub-score is on screen. Delivery weighs most because late material stops a line fastest.

  2. 02

    Safety stock that follows the supplier

    The reorder-point buffer grows with each supplier’s average delay and defect rate, instead of a fixed number of days.

  3. 03

    A simulator that refuses bad pairs

    Splits are allowed only between suppliers in the same category. An invalid pair hides the result instead of showing a misleading saving.

  4. 04

    “AI” that still works without AI

    Supplier letters come from a rule-based template by default; a language model is optional with the user’s own key, so no figure in a letter is invented.

CHECKS & GAPS

The guide’s calculators match the server.

5 backend regression tests and 14 model checks cover invalid imports, invalid split pairs, letter tone, and the penalty and line-stop calculators. The formula calculators in the user guide match the server for all nine suppliers, and the main flow was checked by hand on desktop and a 390 px phone.

  • Not yet: tested with procurement users, a live Gemini call, Safari or Firefox, or a full WCAG audit.
  • Stock is modelled per supplier, not per SKU; MOQ, freight, and supplier capacity are not in the simulation.
Every figure comes from a synthetic factory: 120 purchase orders, 9 suppliers.
