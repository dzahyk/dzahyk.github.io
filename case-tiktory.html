THE HAND-OFFS

Opening a new network point is a chain of hand-offs.

An analyst spots demand, a surveyor checks the street, and a manager approves the spend — usually across spreadsheets and chat. Tiktory keeps the map, the cost estimate, the survey evidence, and the decision in one trail.

Each hand-off has its own question:

UserMain question
AnalystWhere is demand strong enough to justify a new point?
SurveyorIs the location actually buildable?
ManagerIs the estimate sound — approve or defer?
THE APP

Map, survey, decision.

Demand hexagons and a cost simulator for the analyst, a mobile checklist with photo and GPS for the surveyor, and approve-or-defer with an audit history for the manager — plus a landing page that explains the limits of the demo. Its hero frames the illustrated city in a window that opens to the edges as you scroll, with zone labels that use the same words as the map legend in the app.

GUARDRAILS

A demo that does not pretend.

Each decision keeps a demo from looking more certain than it is.

  1. 01

    Server-side roles, even in a demo

    Signed HttpOnly session cookies and role checks on the backend: the wrong role is rejected and cross-origin POSTs are refused.

  2. 02

    Evidence labelled as simulated

    Demo GPS and sample photos are marked as simulations, never as verified presence, and distant coordinates are rejected.

  3. 03

    No duplicate proposals

    A proposal within 100 m, or overlapping an existing coverage circle by more than 30%, is rejected; a retry returns the saved result.

  4. 04

    Payback before operating cost

    The simulator offers conservative, base, and optimistic conversion, and labels payback as before operating cost.

TESTS & LIMITS

31 API tests on a temporary database.

The tests cover session and role checks, cross-site origins, photo privacy, the required checklist, GPS distance and accuracy, approval confirmation, audit, retries, duplicates, coverage overlap, and the ROI model. The full flow was checked in the browser on desktop and a 390 px phone.

  • Not yet: a physical camera and GPS in the field, offline sync, load testing, or organisation sign-in.
  • Hexagons approximate H3 resolution 8 and are not official boundaries. Basemap: Esri World Street Map.
Prototype on simulated data. Not affiliated with any network operator; no real survey or build is claimed.
